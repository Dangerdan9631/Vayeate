# Aries Mini look mechanics

Aries is a rigid armored suit. Its compact helmet and cyan visor lead each look direction by turning at the neck; the dark lower face and central helmet ridge follow as one solid head. The visor is one fixed luminous panel, not a pair of eyes. For up and down, pitch the helmet enough that the visor and chin geometry visibly change. For screen-right and screen-left, yaw the helmet so its visor and face plate point unmistakably toward the corresponding image edge. The chest may follow a little, while the feet and lower body stay planted at one scale and baseline.

The tall twin pale intake housings are mounted to the shoulder/back assembly and remain paired and recognizable. Their apparent width and occlusion can change naturally with upper-body yaw; the wings follow the back and never float away. The dark rifle stays in its gripped hand, swings only a little with upper-body motion, and never swaps hands or detaches. Maintain slate blue armor, pale gray intakes and hip plates, cyan visor, dark rifle and feet, and crisp anime mechanical outlines.

Use even 22.5-degree visual steps. The head turn leads, a slight upper-chest shift follows, and the shoulder housings and rifle lag subtly. Do not rotate, skew, squash, or warp the whole suit. The first and last directions must join smoothly, with the same foot placement and body height throughout.
